Kindred — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-20

This page explains, in plain words, what Kindred does with your family's information. If any sentence here is unclear, that is our mistake to fix, not something you need a lawyer to read for you.

What Kindred stores on your phone

Kindred keeps your family tree — names, relationships, photos, and the record of who confirmed each entry — on your own phone, in an encrypted database. The key that unlocks it is kept in your phone's secure key storage (Keychain on iPhone, Keystore on Android), is not included in phone backups, and is not readable by other apps. There is no central company database holding everyone's family trees. Each family's tree lives on that family's own phones.

What leaves your phone

Nothing, on its own. This version of Kindred keeps your family's records on your phone and does not contact any server. You can use the whole app — build a tree, add people, confirm entries, view the tree — without any of it leaving your device. This version has no backup switch and no online backup. The "Privacy policy" row in Settings opens this page in your phone's web browser, which loads it from our website.

Kindred has no advertising, no analytics tracking, and no crash-reporting service watching how you use the app. Nothing about your usage is collected for those purposes, because none of that code is in the app.

How sharing works

Kindred sends nothing for you. When you share, Kindred hands a file to your phone's share menu, and what happens to it after that is up to you and the app you pick. Three kinds of file can carry your family's information.

  • A family file, which you send so a relative can join or update the family. It is encrypted. A file you export is opened with the family key. The files used to invite and join a relative are locked with a key from the invitation instead.
  • A readable tree page, poster or booklet, which you share so someone can look at the tree. It is not encrypted and needs no key: anyone who gets it can see everything in it. Kindred shows you a notice, "This file is not encrypted", before it shares one, and you can cancel.
  • A recovery sheet, which you save as a PDF or print. It contains your family's key and a QR code for it, in readable form. Whoever holds the key together with a family file can open that family's records. Keep it somewhere safe.

If you copy your family key from the family screen, it is placed on your phone's clipboard in readable form and cleared after 30 seconds if nothing else has replaced it.

Camera and biometric lock

  • Camera: Kindred uses the camera in two places. To read a recovery QR code from the live camera view; nothing from that view is saved or sent. And if you choose "Take photo" on a person's page, the picture you take is kept as that person's photo in your family on your phone, and Kindred does not send it anywhere by itself. A photo leaves your phone only when you share something that includes it. For example, a readable tree page can show a person's main photo as a small picture, and that page is not encrypted: anyone it is forwarded to can see everything in it. You can instead choose a photo from your library. Kindred does not record video or audio.
  • Biometric lock: the optional setting "Require biometrics on launch" uses your phone's own fingerprint or face unlock to open the app. Kindred does not receive or store your fingerprint or face; the check is done by your phone. If your fingerprint or face is not available, your phone may ask for its screen-lock PIN or passcode instead.

Who can see your family's data

People you give something to, anyone they pass it on to, and people who hold your family's key. Someone you send a readable tree page, poster or booklet to does not need the key and can see everything in that file. A recovery sheet or a copied key holds your family's key, and whoever has the key together with a family file can open it. There is no account, no login, and no company employee with a way to read your family's information, because Kindred itself never sends it anywhere. What a person does with a file after you send it is theirs to decide.

Children's information

Kindred is a family-tree app and may include information about children as family members (for example, a child's name and place in the family), entered by an adult relative who is already part of the family's tree. Kindred is not directed at children, does not let a child sign up or use the app on their own account (there are no accounts), and collects nothing beyond what an adult family member chooses to enter about their own family.
